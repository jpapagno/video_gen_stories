# The Room Where Maps Sleep

Prototype narration for review. Headings are not spoken.

## A light beyond the rain

By the time you reach Hogwarts, the rain has softened to a fine silver mist. It rests on the shoulders of your coat and gathers along the edges of the stone steps. Above you, a single window glows the colour of honey.

There is no need to hurry. The door is already open, and warm air reaches you from somewhere within.

You step inside. The entrance hall holds the quiet of a house whose evening work is nearly finished. A few candles float beneath the high ceiling. Their reflections tremble in the polished floor, small and steady as lights seen across still water.

Beside an umbrella stand, someone has placed a folded towel on a wooden stool. You dry your hands. Your coat settles onto a brass hook, and a little household charm begins to lift the rain from its wool.

An elderly wizard waits near the inner doorway. He carries a green-shaded lamp.

Elian Vale introduces himself as the keeper of the seasonal maps. He has saved you a chair, he says, and there is still tea in the pot.

You follow the circle of lamplight into the castle. Behind you, the great door closes with a soft, padded sound. The rain remains outside.

## The room above the lake

The map room is smaller than you expected. A round table stands beneath a narrow window, and shelves of rolled parchment climb almost to the ceiling. Beyond the glass lies the Black Lake, dark and smooth beneath the rain.

Elian sets down the lamp. On the table are two cups, a silver ink bottle, and an atlas bound in faded blue cloth.

These maps, he explains, remember the weather. In the daytime their little roads are busy with painted shadows. At night, they like a few familiar things put gently in order. A window lit. A gate closed. A river given back its stars.

He opens the atlas to an orchard. Between the inked trees, tiny leaves move in a breeze you cannot feel.

You rest your hands around the cup. The tea smells faintly of apples. Somewhere behind the shelves, a clock makes a small, contented click.

Elian leaves a soft brush beside your hand. There is no particular order, he tells you. The orchard can wait until you have finished your tea.

After a while he wishes you a comfortable evening and goes to his own room along the corridor. You hear his footsteps grow quieter. Then there is only the rain, the clock, and the patient light upon the table.

## Silver along the paths

When your tea has cooled, you dip the brush into the silver ink. Only the tip is needed.

You begin with the nearest path. The brush slips over the parchment, leaving a thin line that catches the lamplight. In the orchard, the little trees grow still. A few painted apples settle deeper among their leaves.

There is a cottage at the far edge of the page. You touch the brush to its window, and a warm square of light appears. Beside the cottage, a gate swings gently shut. No one has to come home through the rain now. Everyone in this small landscape is already where they belong.

You put the brush down.

For a while, you simply watch the orchard. Its paths curve between the trees without asking to be followed. Its window shines without needing anyone to look.

Beyond your own window, the lake holds the dark shape of the hills. A little rain slides down the glass. The lamp gives the table its circle of green and gold.

You draw the comfortable chair a little closer. There will be other maps another evening. For now, the orchard is quiet, your hands are warm, and the castle has settled around you.

The page rests beneath the light.

And you rest beside it.
