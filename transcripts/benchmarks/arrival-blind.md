# Blind comparison: arrival

Compare readability aloud, relevant detail, gentle movement, continuity, calm and repetition. Both samples are local drafts, not approved story text.

## Sample 1

You stand beneath the heavy stone arch of Hogwarts' entrance, your breath forming a small white cloud against the damp chill of an autumn evening. The rain falls in steady, soft sheets that have been turning the ground into a dark mirror for hours now. You feel the cold seeping through the thin layers of your blue wool coat, which is already heavy with water droplets from where it passed over your shoulders and arms earlier on the path. It clings to you like a second skin, stiff and wet against your back as you wait under shelter.

Elian Vale appears at the edge of the archway without making much sound. He moves through the falling rain with an ease that suggests he has walked this ground for far longer than any student or guest ever could. His hair is grey and combed into a precise part, his coat a deep charcoal that absorbs the dim light rather than reflecting it. He does not look at you immediately but sweeps up something small from the wet cobblestones with one gloved hand before stepping onto your side of the threshold to let you pass first.

"You are damp," he says softly. His voice is low, lacking any sharp edge or hurry, which makes the words feel more like a quiet fact than an instruction. "The rain does not stop until it has soaked everything."

You nod once, keeping your eyes on his face as you step under the arch and into the dry warmth of the hall. The air changes instantly; the damp chill vanishes to be replaced by something heavy with old stone and wood smoke. You are no longer exposed to the wind anymore, though the sound of rain continues outside in a steady rhythm that vibrates slightly against your chest through the soles of your boots.

Elian waits just beyond the reach of your umbrella until you feel safe moving forward without fear of slipping on wet stones or catching your coat again. He gestures toward an inner door at the far end of the hall, which remains unlatched despite its age and the weight of years upon it. The metal hinges are smooth and oiled, silent under his touch as he nudges them open with a small, careful push that requires no strength but only patience.

He steps back to let you enter first, then follows slowly so that your umbrella can remain inside if you wish. You reach the doorway and pause for just a moment before stepping through into the cool stillness of the main hall beyond. The floor beneath your feet is dry now, polished smooth by generations of footsteps and perhaps some ancient cleaning spells worn down to invisibility over centuries.

As soon as you cross the threshold, Elian gestures with his chin toward a brass hook mounted on the wall near the entrance. You notice immediately that there stands an open towel folded neatly beside it in two clean rectangles, its edges aligned perfectly despite being wet from use moments before. The fabric is thick and absorbent, smelling faintly of lemon and dried lavender when you hold up your damp blue coat to compare against it.

You lift the heavy garment free from your shoulders with one hand while setting down the umbrella carefully on a nearby table where it clicks softly into place. You do not shake out your coat immediately; instead, you fold it slowly in two neat halves before laying both layers flat over the towel's surface so that every inch of fabric dries evenly without running cold water directly onto anything else in the hall.

The movement feels simple and natural, requiring no magic or complicated incantations to achieve such a basic result. Elian watches from the doorway as you work his fold with steady hands, offering nothing more than an approving nod when your task is complete. He has done much of this many times before; he knows exactly how long it takes for water to drain out properly without dripping back onto the floor or your boots again.

"You have taken care," Elian says finally, stepping aside fully so that you may proceed further into the hall if you wish now. "It is good work."

He speaks not as a command but as an observation of something already done well. There is no rush to hurry away from this quiet space; there is only the slow drying of your clothes and the settling of your body against the warmth of stone walls that have absorbed rain for days without complaint. The hall stretches out before you, vast and empty except for a few scattered objects like dust motes dancing in shafts of high window light and a single potted fern growing slowly on its own schedule near a pillar.

You feel heavier now with your folded coat settled at the hook where it belongs, lighter in places that were stiff from moisture but ready to be worn again later when the rain stops or you move indoors completely. The sound outside has shifted slightly; perhaps more clouds have moved across the sky, making the rainfall softer still as if nature itself is lowering its volume for your comfort here within these ancient walls.

Elian watches one last time before turning away from you toward the end of the hall where shadows pool deep against the floorboards and wood grain patterns run parallel along their length like rivers flowing backward in reverse time. He does not say another word; he knows that you need only to exist safely here without needing anything more than what is already present around you now.

The air remains still, carrying no smell of fear or urgency but rather the faint scent of old paper and cedar oil mixed together from somewhere deeper within this place where time moves slower and less urgently than outside under the grey sky above. Your hands are dry now; your feet feel solid beneath them once more as you take another step forward into the quiet heart of Hogwarts without thinking about where exactly it leads next or when anything will ever change again tonight.


## Sample 2

The rain hits your face like a cold sheet of water that demands you stand still to let it soak in before anything else happens. You turn slightly so the blue wool coat can catch more of the downpour, feeling the dampness already seeping through the fabric against your chest and shoulders. It feels heavy and unkind under your arms as if the sky is trying to weigh itself onto every step you take toward the castle walls. The ground beneath your feet slicks with mud that reflects the gray light above in broken shards of rain-slicked pavement.

You reach out a hand toward the stone archway ahead, fingers brushing against the rough texture of ancient masonry that has weathered centuries without complaint. There is no lock here to turn or handle to grip; the door stands open wide as an empty mouth waiting for you to step into its shadow. The air inside shifts instantly from wet and heavy outside to still and dry inside, carrying with it a faint scent of old stone and dried oak leaves rather than rainwater. You swing your legs over the threshold without thinking twice about where your boots will land next.

The transition from out there to in here feels like slipping into deep water where you can breathe again once the surface breaks below you. Your coat is still dripping when you lift it up, but the sudden dryness of the hall makes every drop seem insignificant compared to what lies ahead of you now. You look down at your hands and see them covered in tiny beads that roll away from your fingertips as if eager to escape your skin entirely.

Elian Vale stands just beyond the archway where two torches cast their orange glow across his face like a second light source added deliberately by someone who knows how things should be arranged inside this place. He watches you without speaking at first, letting the silence stretch long enough for you to notice that he is holding something small and flat in one hand while keeping the other free near his side where it might reach for assistance if needed later on.

"You've made quite a run through the mud," he says finally when your boots click once against the floor tiles below him. The coat hangs now on its hook without any help from your hands or muscles trying too hard to lift it up again after carrying all that weight through mud and rain together until you found this place exactly as Elian knew it would end today if nothing else changed about how storms behave outside these walls protecting lives inside them one life at a time. You step forward carefully watching where each foot lands next feeling grounded suddenly in dry earth beneath your shoes instead of the slippery surface that tried to pull you backward toward cold water earlier moments ago now forgotten behind closed doors leading further into this building called home for all who need it most when weather turns against them unexpectedly again and again without warning or mercy from nature itself wanting only what comes next after every drop falls down where it belongs waiting patiently in pools formed by puddles created during storms before drying up under suns that do not exist anymore here at night.
