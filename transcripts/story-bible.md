# The Room Where Maps Sleep — story bible v1

Original, unofficial fan fiction set at Hogwarts, with new principal characters and an original plot. Use an independent narrative voice. The atlas room, its custodian, and its magic are inventions for this story.

## Premise

Rowan is an adult magical cartographer visiting Hogwarts during a quiet autumn holiday to tend its seasonal landscape maps. Over one rainy evening, he settles several small enchanted landscapes for the night. Elian Vale, the elderly custodian, welcomes him and explains the work. Care, patient observation, and simple household magic bring each task to a comfortable conclusion. The evening ends in a guest room; the narration gradually settles without awakening the listener.

## Locked facts

- Narrator: third person, present tense, following Rowan (he/him), an adult visitor, not a student. Third person was selected by the user after measuring the reference. Earlier second-person drafts are retained as historical experiments.
- Named principal characters: Rowan, visiting cartographer; Elian Vale, elderly male map custodian, warm, practical and understated.
- Time/weather: one autumn evening continuing into night; fine rain, no thunder or sudden weather change.
- Clothing: blue wool coat, damp at arrival, dried on an entrance-hall brass hook, collected on the final walk to the guest room.
- Objects: a green-shaded brass lamp; a faded-blue-cloth atlas; silver ink; a soft brush; apple-scented tea; brass map-cabinet key supplied by Elian during the welcome.
- Geography: entrance hall → small tea room → map room above the Black Lake → portrait corridor → guest room.
- Elian departs after orienting Rowan in the map room. No surprise return or new conversation with him later.
- Magic: maps contain tiny, gently moving painted landscapes. A small amount of silver ink helps familiar details settle at night. No portal travel, sentient threat, prophecy, or secret puzzle.
- Guests may use ordinary household charms; every activity is safe, voluntary, and unhurried.
- Key/coat are not already in the guest room before they are carried there. The guest-room door remains an ordinary comfortable door.

## Narrative voice

Plain, warm prose intended to be read aloud. Mix short and medium sentences, allowing occasional longer sentences that stay easy to follow. Sensory details should arise from something the visitor touches, tends, watches, or hears. Use a small amount of ordinary dialogue. Let people reveal kindness through practical actions.

Every scene has a small task or transition. A description supports that movement. Avoid a catalogue of cozy objects or paragraphs that restate the same mood. Let quiet satisfaction replace suspense.

The early chapters can introduce details. The last quarter should reduce novelty and dialogue. The final chapter introduces no new character, task, magical rule, destination, or promise of an adventure tomorrow.

## Boundaries

No danger, villain, chase, violence, examinations, deadlines, threatening mystery, cliffhanger, loud surprise, or dramatic spell. No waking the listener at the end. No commands to sleep, breathing exercises, or health claims. Do not retell a book/film plot, borrow dialogue, or imitate a particular author's prose. Familiar Hogwarts locations and ordinary magical details can anchor the setting; the new maps and characters carry the story.

Avoid repeated assurances such as 'there is nothing you need to do' and 'there is no need to hurry.' Show safety once through the situation instead of explaining it in every scene. Avoid ending every scene with a lamp, rain, warmth, or 'the castle around you.' Repeated motifs are welcome when their meaning or use develops.
