You walk the last few paces beneath the stone arch, the rain heavy against your shoulders. The blue wool coat feels damp and heavy on your skin. You feel the cool air seeping through the fabric as you move forward. The sound of the rain changes here. It becomes softer, less frantic, as the wind stops its howl. You hear only the steady drumming on the slate roof above.

Your boots click softly on the wet stones as you approach the inner door. It is a simple wooden thing, dark and smooth, standing against the wall. The latch is loose, a small metal bar resting gently in its place. You do not touch it yet. You stand still, letting the rhythm of the rain settle into your bones.

The stone entrance hall waits beyond, quiet and dry. The air smells faintly of old dust and dried leaves. Your breath fogs slightly in the cooler air near the frame. You take another small step, feeling the solid wood beneath your hand if you were to touch it. The door remains closed but ready to open.

You stand sheltered beside the wooden inner door, your coat still worn and damp. The rain continues its slow, steady drumming just outside the frame. There is no rush, no noise, only the quiet presence of the evening. The world feels still and safe around you. You wait, listening to the world breathe.
