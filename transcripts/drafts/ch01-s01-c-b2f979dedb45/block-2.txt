You reach for the brass pull on the door. Your fingers wrap around its cool metal surface. You turn the latch gently, feeling the mechanism click softly in your palm. The wood groans a little under your weight as you begin to push. The barrier between outside and inside dissolves into warm stone air.

The door swings open, revealing the dry entrance hall beyond. You step inside, leaving the rain behind on the threshold. The heavy blue coat feels lighter now, though still damp against your sides. Your boots make no sound on the clean, dry stone floor. It is smooth and cool under your weight.

You let the heavy wooden door close behind you. It settles with a soft thud that vibrates through your soles. The rain is gone, replaced by the stillness of the hall. Several candles float inches above the floor, burning without a visible flame. They drift slowly, casting long, dancing shadows against the white walls.

You stand just inside the frame, looking out at the floating lights. The air smells faintly of old parchment and dried leaves. Your hands remain damp from the rain and the brass handle. You do not dry them or seek a cloth. The candles hum a low, steady tune that matches your breathing.

The hall holds its breath around you, waiting for nothing. The floating lights drift closer, their glow warming your face. There is no one else here, only you and the quiet magic of the room. The evening feels safe and complete, wrapped in soft, steady light. You stand still, letting the calm fill your chest.
