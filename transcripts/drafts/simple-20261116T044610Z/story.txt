# The Keeper of the Whispering Hearth

The village of Eldergate did not appear on most maps, though it was found on none the less; it existed in the gentle fold between the mundane and the magical, a place where the fog clung to the cobblestones with a reluctance that felt almost affectionate. It was a Tuesday evening in late October, the sort of time when the air turns crisp enough to catch on the back of one's neck, carrying with it the scent of woodsmoke, damp earth, and the faint, sweet perfume of honeysuckle that had somehow survived the chill.

In the heart of Eldergate stood the school of St. Aethelgard's, not a place of towering spires or imposing stone walls, but a collection of cottages clustered tightly around a central courtyard where the ivy grew thick and ancient. The building itself was a patchwork of eras, its bricks varied in colour from burnt orange to soft grey, the timber beams of the upper floors swaying slightly in the evening breeze, a testament to the subtle magic that held the structure together long after the mortar had worn thin.

At the far end of the village, in a cottage whose roof had been patched with tiles that shimmered like captured moonlight, lived Elara. She was a woman of moderate years, with hair the colour of dried wheat that she kept tied back in a messy bun secured by a wooden hairpin shaped like a small oak leaf. Her hands were warm and calloused, the result of years spent tending the hearth, baking bread, and mending the small, everyday enchantments that kept Eldergate running smoothly.

Elara was a Keeper of the Hearth. This was a role that most outsiders dismissed as quaint, a bit like a librarian or a postman, but to those who understood the rhythm of the village, it was of profound importance. The Hearth was not merely the fire in the centre of the common room; it was the central nervous system of the school's magic.
