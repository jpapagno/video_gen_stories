# Adult sleep-story format: first-party source notes

Reviewed 2026-11-15. These are creator-described craft conventions and product formats, not evidence that any textual metric causes better sleep. No story prose is reproduced.

## Four useful primary sources

1. [Calm: The art of writing sleep stories](https://www.calm.com/blog/the-thinking-behind-sleep-stories). Interview with Calm writer/editor/producer Chris Advansun. **Creator description:** simple character and desire; sensory observation; an opening body scan; breath/body awareness within the narrative; cumulative sentences and parallel structures that can become repetitive; tension and obstacles are minimized while the experience becomes calmer. The endpoint is contentment, safety, gratitude, and relaxation. Slow, melodic performance matters separately from writing. **Availability:** craft interview, not a story script; its paragraph lengths cannot serve as story measurements. **Implication:** useful support for an opening settling segment, simple gentle progression, sensory elaboration, and a calm endpoint. No numeric sentence, paragraph, or repetition targets are specified.

2. [Nothing Much Happens: official home page](https://www.nothingmuchhappens.com/). **Creator description:** Kathryn Nicolai writes and reads the bedtime stories; each is performed twice, with the second reading slower. **Availability:** descriptive website copy and audio links, not an authored transcript. **Implication:** repeated audio is an explicit house format. Do not count the second telling as new story writing, or infer that longer runtime requires proportionally more unique prose. This is one creator's convention, not a universal adult sleep-story rule.

3. [Nothing Much Happens: Slightly More Happens — May Mischief](https://www.nothingmuchhappens.com/stories/slightly-more-happens-may-mischief). **Creator description:** the official episode description identifies a sequence of three activities involving a recurring character: flower-related mischief, house restoration, and a market visit. **Availability:** episode metadata, short synopsis, embedded player, and a site link advertising YouTube subtitles; no full story transcript in the fetched page. **Implication:** multi-story/episodic packaging exists alongside the repeated-telling format above. The synopsis does not establish whether each segment is newly written or reused, its exact runtime, or how a particular long YouTube upload is assembled. Check that upload's official description/timestamps before labeling it a loop, compilation, or continuous original.

4. [Slumber Studios: Get Sleepy episode guide](https://slumberstudios.com/blog/get-sleepy-episode-guide/) (updated September 2, 2026). **Creator description:** a host welcome, slow settling breaths, then softly delivered story; almost all stories stand alone, with occasional two-part stories. Curated collections include trains, rain, oceans, and myths. Premium includes extra-long soundscapes. **Availability:** official format guide, not story transcripts; collection names and descriptions are not a measurable prose corpus. **Implication:** distinguish welcome/settling/story from one another. Extra-long audio can include soundscape material; this source does not establish looping in any particular video.

## Practical measurement plan (our inference, not creator rules)

- Label `welcome`, `settling`, `story`, `ending`, `promotion`, `repeat_pass`, and `soundscape` separately before computing a reference profile. Advertisements and introductions can distort sentence and word distributions.
- Count unique story words separately from total spoken words and runtime. Report repeated-pass count and repeated-word fraction when an actual transcript/audio supports them.
- For story-only text, measure sentence word-count median and percentiles, dialogue share, new named entities/locations per 500 words, scene transitions, and lexical repetition. Segment the story into equal word windows to inspect whether new people/places and events decrease toward its end. These are descriptive engineering candidates, not validated sleep-quality scores.
- Use a small explicit beat plan for a cheap model: settle; enter one simple place/activity; observe and perform small benign actions; revisit sensory motifs; finish in rest/contentment. This synthesizes the sources; none mandates this exact five-beat template or numeric proportions.
- Do not impose very short sentences or a rigid maximum from intuition alone: Calm specifically describes long cumulative sentences as one legitimate technique. Measure varied references before choosing constraints.

## Transcript and paragraph-boundary caution

None of these four fetched primary pages supplies a complete author-formatted sleep-story script. Therefore there is **no defensible paragraph-length baseline from this research set**. Official captions/subtitles can be useful for spoken wording, after checking corrections and ads, but caption line breaks/timing chunks do not demonstrate the writer's paragraphs. ASR punctuation also needs provenance and quality labels. Record `text_origin`, `punctuation_origin`, and `paragraph_origin` for each future sample. Only creator-published full written stories/scripts with preserved formatting should supply paragraph distributions. Treat third-party transcript reflow as unknown, even when words resemble the episode.

Long-form conclusion: first-party evidence confirms repeated tellings for Nothing Much Happens, episode collections/multiple segments, and extra-long soundscapes for Get Sleepy. It does **not** justify a blanket claim that all long sleep videos loop stories, or that hours-long videos contain hours of new continuous prose. This distinction must be verified per recording.
